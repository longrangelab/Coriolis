Two models make the call. This one learns from the wind alone, no shots. Every second it predicts each meter's wind 2 to 20 s ahead, checks each prediction when that wind arrives, learns from the miss, and keeps trying new models and Sentinel pairs, keeping what predicts best. Where the training goes
Share of the shot is how much of the call rests on each meter: its spot on the path, weighted by how much a crosswind there moves the bullet (wind near the muzzle counts most), times its learned multiplier. A Sentinel earns part of the share of the meter it warns. Meters with a bigger share get a bigger pool and try two new models every generation.
Model pool for this meter
The forecast is a blend of the three best, weighted by how small their recent misses are.
Sentinel pairs proven by prediction
A pair is proven when the best model for a meter leans on another meter's reading and beats every model without it by at least 10%. One that is not upwind still helps the forecast (big gusts and swings cover several meters) but makes a poor Wind Lab Sentinel; Wind Lab's pairs come from Relations, which demands an upwind leader.
What it knows, wind by wind
Every wind direction and speed band keeps its own models. When the wind turns, the ones for the wind it left are put away and the ones it learned for the new wind come back to compete, so a Sentinel pair that only works in a north wind is ready the next time the wind comes from the north. Skill is how much of the error of "the wind stays as it is" it removes 10 s ahead, averaged over the meters.
What it tried
How the meters move together, learned second by second from their 1 s wind readings. A meter that sees the same gusts a few seconds before another is an early warning for it: that is what a Sentinel is. Every wind direction is learned on its own, because who is upwind changes with it.

Who warns the whole field
Each meter against the rest of the field (the average gust of every other meter). A meter that warns the field sees a gust before the rest get it, so a change there tells you what most of the range is about to do. Moves with the field: it reads what the field reads, at the same moment. On its own: its gusts are its own. Direction shifts: the same question for the wind turning, so a meter that turns first tells you where the rest are about to swing. The wind model also tries a model on every meter at once for each meter, so these field-wide patterns feed its predictions.
Sentinel pairs this wind supports
The Sentinel's reading from lag seconds ago, times the speed ratio, is what the paired meter should read now. Layout pairs come from the meters' spots alone (the meter straight upwind), so they work from the first minute, with wide tolerances; a wind data pair replaces one as soon as the 1 s readings back it, with tolerances from how far it normally strays. Predicts: the wind model has proven it. The Model tab sends these to Wind Lab.
